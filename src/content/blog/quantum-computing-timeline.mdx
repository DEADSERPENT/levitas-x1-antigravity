# The Quantum Computing Timeline: From Theory to Anti-Gravity

The development of quantum flux pinning technology didn't happen overnight. It's the culmination of over a century of scientific progress, from the earliest quantum theories to today's practical anti-gravity applications.

## The Early Days: 1900-1950

The story begins with Max Planck's revolutionary quantum hypothesis in 1900. His idea that energy comes in discrete packets, or "quanta," laid the groundwork for everything that followed.

> "Science cannot solve the ultimate mystery of nature. And that is because, in the last analysis, we ourselves are a part of the mystery that we are trying to solve." — Max Planck

### Key Milestones

- **1900**: Planck introduces quantum theory
- **1911**: Discovery of superconductivity by Heike Kamerlingh Onnes
- **1933**: Meissner effect discovered
- **1950**: Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity

## The Superconductor Era: 1950-1986

The discovery of Type II superconductors opened new possibilities. Unlike Type I superconductors, these materials could trap magnetic flux in quantized vortices—the phenomenon we now call "flux pinning."

```
Flux Pinning Strength = Jc × B × V

Where:
- Jc = Critical current density
- B = Magnetic field strength
- V = Volume of superconductor
```

## High-Temperature Revolution: 1986-2010

The discovery of high-temperature superconductors (HTS) in 1986 was transformative. Suddenly, superconductivity was achievable at temperatures obtainable with liquid nitrogen rather than liquid helium.

### The YBCO Breakthrough

Yttrium barium copper oxide (YBCO) became the material of choice:

- Critical temperature: 92K (-181°C)
- Strong flux pinning characteristics
- Practical for real-world applications
